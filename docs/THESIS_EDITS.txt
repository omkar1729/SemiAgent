[B-BACKBONE]  (Methodology / System Architecture — Diagnosis Agent)
The Diagnosis Agent is a retrieval-augmented LLM that synthesizes the detected defect
descriptor, the retrieved technical passages, and any optional engineer observation
into a ranked set of root-cause hypotheses with explicit chain-of-thought reasoning
and source citations. Its primary backbone is GPT-4o-mini, accessed through a
structured chain-of-thought prompt that emits a fixed JSON schema (summary, ranked
hypotheses with cited evidence, and investigation parameters). A domain-specialized
open model, SemiKong-8B (pentagoniac/SEMIKONG-8b-GPTQ, served via vLLM), is evaluated
as an alternative backbone rather than as the default, allowing a direct comparison
between a general-purpose frontier model and a semiconductor-specialized one under
identical retrieval and prompting conditions.

[B-DETECTOR]  (Methodology / System Architecture — Detection Agent)
The Detection Agent classifies each 52x52 wafer bin map across the 38 MixedWM38
categories. It is implemented by fine-tuning a Vision Transformer (ViT-Base,
google/vit-base-patch16-224) on the training split, with a CLIP zero-shot classifier
and an untrained-ViT variant retained as controls. This design follows the same
single-network classification paradigm validated on MixedWM38 by WaferSegClassNet and
ViT-Tiny (reported at 98.2% accuracy and 98.4% F1), and is benchmarked against them on
a comparable macro-F1 basis.

[B-RETRIEVAL]  (Methodology / System Architecture — Knowledge Retrieval Agent; corpus)
The Knowledge Retrieval Agent indexes a corpus of approximately 1,000 public technical
documents — 776 arXiv paper abstracts, 200 semiconductor-manufacturing patent texts
(via Google Patents), and 24 hand-authored seed abstracts (three per single-defect
type). The seed abstracts are concise, synthetically written defect-mechanism
summaries created within this project to guarantee per-type coverage; they are not
externally expert-validated. Retrieval is hybrid: a dense semantic index (Chroma,
all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings) and a sparse lexical index (BM25) are combined with
Reciprocal Rank Fusion. The seed abstracts additionally serve as the relevance ground
truth for the retrieval evaluation; because they are both members of the corpus and
the relevance labels, that evaluation measures recovery of known-relevant planted
documents rather than fully independent human relevance judgments.

[B-DATAPREP]  (Methodology / Data Preparation)
The raw MixedWM38 archive encodes each wafer as an 8-dimensional multi-hot vector over
the base defect types, which we convert to the 38-class label space used throughout.
The mapping from the eight columns to defect types follows the dataset authors'
official ordering and was verified directly against the dataset's documented
per-pattern index ranges (e.g., rows 25000-25999 = Edge-Loc, 37015-38014 = Scratch),
ensuring the label conversion is exact. The 38,015 maps were split 70/15/15
(26,610 / 5,702 / 5,703) with stratification on the primary class.

[B-CORRECTNESS]  (Methodology / Evaluation — Diagnostic Correctness)
Diagnostic report quality is scored with an LLM-as-judge rubric: a grader model
assigns a 1-5 score on five frozen dimensions (causal correctness, evidence grounding,
technical accuracy, completeness, and actionability), averaged and rescaled to a 0-10
scale. Because the default grader (GPT-4o-mini) shares a model family with the default
writer, the rubric's validity is established through a multi-judge cross-check
(Experiment 4) in which the same reports are independently re-graded by Claude, Gemini,
and SemiKong. Evaluation by human domain experts was outside the scope of this thesis
and is identified as future work.

[B-TARGETS]  (Significance/Intro — the ">85% demonstrating practical utility" sentence)
The system was designed to meet a detection-accuracy target above 90% and a
retrieval-relevance target above 85%. The detection target was exceeded (98.4%);
retrieval relevance reached 0.671 (cosine) / 0.45-0.53 (P@5), below the target, a gap
discussed in the Limitations as a consequence of the small judged query set and
overlapping defect semantics rather than a deficiency of the hybrid design.

[B-LIMITATIONS]  (Limitations)
Several limitations qualify these results. First, diagnostic quality was assessed by an
LLM-as-judge rubric rather than by human domain experts; the risk of same-family bias
was tested directly and rejected (four independent graders agree on the GPT > SemiKong
ranking), but absolute scores remain grader-specific and human expert validation is
left to future work. Second, while detection was evaluated across all 38 classes, the
diagnosis and ablation studies were conducted on the eight single-defect classes and
on modest sample sizes (10-50 wafers); the large effects (removing the LLM or
retrieval) are significant at every size, but the smaller effects (sparse-only
retrieval, SemiKong) only reach significance from N=30 onward and should be read
accordingly. Third, retrieval relevance did not reach the 85% target on the limited
judged query set, and that ground truth (the seed abstracts) is not independent human
judgment, reflecting the semantic similarity among defect descriptions rather than a
failure of the retrieval architecture.

  Narrative: The fine-tuned ViT-Base reached 98.4% top-1 accuracy and 0.982 macro-F1 on
  500 held-out test maps (100% on each single-defect class), exceeding the >90% target
  and matching the published 98.2% WaferSegClassNet benchmark on a macro-F1 basis; the
  CLIP zero-shot and untrained-ViT controls scored 5.0% and 4.4% (0.008 macro-F1),
  confirming that wafer maps are far out-of-distribution for general image models.
  Retrieval relevance fell short of the >85% target: the hybrid retriever returned a
  mean cosine similarity of 0.671 and Precision@5 of 0.45 (with sparse BM25 marginally
  ahead at 0.53). This shortfall is attributable to the small judged query set and the
  semantic overlap between defect descriptions (e.g., Donut and Edge-Ring are both
  ring-shaped) and did not materially limit downstream diagnosis. End-to-end processing
  took 4.56 s per wafer, ~98% of it the LLM generation call; detection and retrieval
  were negligible, indicating throughput is bounded by the language-model service
  rather than local hardware.

  jointly on the language model's reasoning and the retrieved evidence, not on detection.

[INSERT after Table 4 — Diagnostic-factor analysis]
Across a fully crossed detector x engineer-note grid (N=50), the detector choice
produced only marginal differences in report quality (marginal means: CLIP 9.20,
trained ViT 9.50, untrained ViT 9.65). The counter-intuitive edge for the untrained
detector is an artifact of the evaluation: an untrained model emits a consistent
(incorrect) label, the LLM then produces a coherent, well-grounded report for that
label, and — absent an engineer note — nothing exposes the error to the grader, which
rewards coherence and citation rather than label correctness. Adding a defect-accurate
engineer note slightly reduced the mean score (no-note 9.58 vs. note 9.32, p<0.001),
driven by the conflict the note creates when the detected label is wrong. On the
backbone comparison, GPT-4o-mini outscored SemiKong-8B (9.60 vs. 9.33, p<0.001); to
rule out an unfair-context confound (SemiKong's 3,072-token window required truncated
documents), GPT-4o-mini was re-evaluated on the same truncated documents and still led
(9.51 vs. 9.33, p=0.008), while truncation itself had no significant effect on
GPT-4o-mini (9.60 vs. 9.51, p=0.16).

[INSERT after Table 4 — End-to-end latency, one line]
End-to-end processing averaged 4.56 s per wafer, decomposed as detection 0.09 s,
retrieval 0.02 s, and diagnosis (LLM) 4.45 s — roughly 98% of the total — so system
responsiveness is bounded by the language-model service, not local computation.

[INSERT after Table 5 — Experiment 4: Multi-judge validity check]
To test whether the LLM-as-judge rubric favored its own model family, 20 wafers were
each written up by both GPT-4o-mini and SemiKong-8B, and every report was independently
re-scored by four graders (GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and
SemiKong-8B) using the identical rubric. All four graders ranked GPT-authored reports
above SemiKong-authored reports — GPT-4o-mini +0.40 (p=0.001), Claude +0.60 (p=0.004),
Gemini +0.86 (p=0.004), and SemiKong +0.44 (p=0.063). Notably, the GPT-4o-mini grader
exhibited the smallest pro-GPT margin, and SemiKong, grading its own outputs, still
preferred the GPT-authored reports. Graders differed in absolute strictness (mean
scores: Gemini 9.49, GPT-4o-mini 9.44, Claude 8.80, SemiKong 5.22), so absolute rubric
values are grader-specific; however, the relative GPT > SemiKong ranking is robust
across independent judges, confirming the diagnostic-quality comparison is not an
artifact of self-grading.
